## Monday 12 — `NaiveEngine`

### Prediction (before measuring)

1. **Sign of the gap:** positive, because more debits than credits are likely to be lost.
2. **Compared with week 1 (+5.9 to +8.2%):** larger, because there are more concurrent transfers, hence more lost debits.
3. **10 or 1,000 accounts:** the gap will be larger with 10 accounts, because the chance of a collision drops as the number of accounts grows.

### Method

`EngineLab --engine=naive --threads=1000 --transfers=1000 --virtual --work=20 --hot=50 --audit`, 5 runs with `--accounts=10`, then 5 with `--accounts=1000`.

⚠️ These are not the planned conditions (platform threads, no work, no hot account):

- `--work=20` had **no effect**: `NaiveEngine.transfer` does not call `Work.simulate()`.
- `--hot=50` sends half of the transfers to account 0, **whatever the number of accounts**. The 10 vs 1,000 comparison is therefore skewed.

### Findings

- `BROKEN` in 10 runs out of 10, and the gap is **always positive**.
- As a percentage, the gap is hundreds of times larger than in week 1.
- In **absolute** terms, the gap is about the same with 10 and 1,000 accounts (roughly 0.9 to 2.4 M). Only the percentage changes, because the initial total is 100 times larger.
- During the run, the sum dropped **below** the initial total (minimum 40,458 for 50,000). This is not necessarily a lost credit: `total()` reads the accounts one by one, without a lock, and can land between the debit and the credit of a transfer in progress.

### Experiment: without the balance check

**Hypothesis:** debits are lost more often because their window runs from `if (balances[from] < amount)` to the write (the JIT may reuse the value read in the check). Without the `if`, debit and credit have the same window, so the sign of the gap should become random.

**Method:** `if` commented out in `NaiveEngine`, `--threads=1000 --transfers=1000 --audit`, 5 runs per account count. The `if` was restored afterwards.

| Accounts | Gaps (5 runs) | Sign |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | −10,401; +19,209; +7,653; −13,261; −48,604 | **random** (3 negative, 2 positive) |
| 10 | +1,734,453; +289,806; +264,361; +484,489; +580,140 | **always positive** |

**Findings:**

- With 1,000 accounts, the hypothesis holds: without the check, the sign becomes random.
- With 10 accounts, the gap stays positive: the balance check does not explain the whole asymmetry under extreme contention. **Open question.** Next experiment: swap the order (credit, then debit). If the gap turns negative, the order of the writes is what matters.
- Without the check, balances go below zero: the sum read during the run dropped to −293,910.
