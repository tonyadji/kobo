## Monday 12 — `NaiveEngine`

### Prediction (before measuring)

1. **Sign of the gap:** positive, because more debits than credits are likely to be lost.
2. **Compared with week 1 (+5.9 to +8.2%):** larger, because there are more concurrent transfers, hence more lost debits.
3. **10 or 1,000 accounts:** the gap will be larger with 10 accounts, because the chance of a collision drops as the number of accounts grows.

### Method

`EngineLab --engine=naive --threads=1000 --transfers=1000 --virtual --work=20 --hot=50 --audit`, 5 runs with `--accounts=10`, then 5 with `--accounts=1000`.

⚠️ These are not the planned conditions (platform threads, no work, no hot account):

- `--work=20` had **no effect**: `NaiveEngine.transfer` does not call `Work.simulate()`.
- `--hot=50` sends half of the transfers to account 0, **whatever the number of accounts**. The 10 vs 1,000 comparison is therefore skewed.

### Experiment: without the balance check

**Hypothesis:** debits are lost more often because their window runs from `if (balances[from] < amount)` to the write (the JIT may reuse the value read in the check). Without the `if`, debit and credit have the same window, so the sign of the gap should become random.

**Method:** `if` commented out in `NaiveEngine`, `--threads=1000 --transfers=1000 --audit`, 5 runs per account count. The `if` was restored afterwards.

| Accounts | Gaps (5 runs) | Sign |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | −10,401; +19,209; +7,653; −13,261; −48,604 | **random** (3 negative, 2 positive) |
| 10 | +1,734,453; +289,806; +264,361; +484,489; +580,140 | **always positive** |

**Findings:**

- With 1,000 accounts, the hypothesis holds: without the check, the sign becomes random.
- With 10 accounts, the gap stays positive: the balance check does not explain the whole asymmetry under extreme contention. **Open question.** Next experiment: swap the order (credit, then debit). If the gap turns negative, the order of the writes is what matters.
- Without the check, balances go below zero: the sum read during the run dropped to −293,910.

## Tuesday 13 — `GlobalLockEngine`

The whole body of `transfer` runs inside `synchronized (lock)`, and so does `total()`. `Work.simulate()` is called only after a successful transfer: a rejected transfer (insufficient funds) holds the lock for almost no time.

### Prediction (before measuring)

| Accounts | `--work` | `--transfers` | Predicted duration |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 10 | 0 | 1,000 | not written (to be done before measuring) |
| 1,000 | 0 | 1,000 | not written |
| 10 | 20 µs | 100 | 2 s |
| 1,000 | 20 µs | 100 | 2.5 s |

Theoretical ceiling of a single lock: 1 / critical-section duration = 1 / 20 µs = **50,000 transfers/s**, whatever the number of threads or cores.

### Method

`EngineLab --engine=globalLock --threads=1000 --audit`, with `--accounts` = 10 or 1,000, `--work` = 0 or 20, `--transfers` = 100 or 1,000 (the guide planned `--transfers=100` with `--work=20`; the 1,000 runs were added to compare). 3 runs each, median kept.

⚠️ `--audit` was on for every run: the auditor takes the lock every millisecond. The impact is small, but all engines must be measured under the same conditions this week.

### Checking the instrument: how long does `parkNanos(20 µs)` really take?

**Prediction:** about 30 µs.

**Method:** 10,000 × `LockSupport.parkNanos(20_000)` in a single thread, no lock.

**Result:** **28.1 µs per park**, 8 µs more than requested.

| | µs |
| --- | ---: |
| One `parkNanos(20_000)`, alone | 28.1 |
| One accepted transfer under the global lock (`--transfers=1000`) | ≈ 29 |
| **Cost of handing the lock over** | **≈ 1** |

### Findings

- **The number of accounts makes no difference to throughput** (≈ 33,000–34,500/s with 10 or 1,000 accounts). Every transfer queues on the same lock, whatever accounts it touches. Runs with 1,000 accounts last longer only because they do more useful work: almost no `from == to` skipped (≈ 10% with 10 accounts), so ≈ 909,000 accepted transfers instead of ≈ 823,000.
- **Measured: ≈ 34,000/s, against a computed ceiling of 50,000/s.** The formula holds; the input was wrong: on this Mac, `--work=20` really costs ≈ 28 µs. Real ceiling: 1 / 28.1 µs ≈ 35,600/s, so the global lock reaches **≈ 97% of its real ceiling**.
- **Handing the lock over costs ≈ 1 µs**, even with 1,000 waiting threads. Almost all of the critical section is the work done under the lock.
- **Without work, the lock itself costs ≈ 0.2 µs per transfer** (≈ 4.6 M/s). Runs of 140–200 ms are too short to be precise (JIT warm-up, 30% spread): an order of magnitude only.
- **The sum read during the run is always exact**, because `total()` takes the same lock as `transfer` (compare with `NaiveEngine`: minimum 40,458 for 50,000).
- **Known bias for the whole week:** `--work=20` means ≈ 28 µs on this machine. It applies to every engine, so comparisons stay valid, but ceilings must be computed with 28 µs.
- Longer runs are more stable: ≈ 4–5% spread with `--transfers=1000`, up to 11% with `--transfers=100`.

### Prediction check

| Prediction | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 10 accounts, `--work=20`: 2 s | ❌ 2.5 s (+26%): the prediction used 20 µs per transfer; the real cost is ≈ 30 µs |
| 1,000 accounts, `--work=20`: 2.5 s (longer than with 10 accounts) | ✅ on the direction, ❌ on the value: 3.0 s (+19%) |
| `parkNanos(20 µs)` takes ≈ 30 µs | ✅ 28.1 µs |

**Reference for the week:** a finer-grained lock is only worth it if it beats ≈ 34,000/s with `--work=20` and ≈ 4.6 M/s without work.

## Wednesday 14 — Per-account locks and the deadlock

`UnorderedLockEngine` and `OrderedLockEngine`: `long[] balances` and one `ReentrantLock` per account (`ReentrantLock[] locks`, built with `Arrays.setAll`). The unordered engine locks `from`, then `to`. The ordered engine locks `min(from, to)`, then `max(from, to)`: one place decides the order (ADR-001 rule). `total()` takes no lock: it is not a consistent snapshot while transfers run (to be measured with `--audit` on Thursday).

### Bugs caught before measuring

Found in code review, before any measured run. Each one would have made the measurements meaningless.

| # | Bug | Effect | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `synchronized (balances[from])` on a `Long[]` | Locks the *value*, not the account. `Long` is immutable: `balances[from] -= amount` puts a **new** object in the slot, so two threads can "hold account 1" at the same time. `Arrays.fill` put **the same** `Long` in every slot at start (boxed once), and `Long.valueOf` shares objects between −128 and 127. | Separate data and locks: `long[]` + one lock object per account, never replaced |
| 2 | Order based on balances (`balances[from] < balances[to]`) | The order changes with every transfer and is read without a lock: two threads can see two different orders for the same pair | Order by account id |
| 3 | `total()` locked the array (`synchronized (balances)`) | No transfer takes that lock: no exclusion at all | No lock, documented (see above) |
| 4 | `Arrays.fill(locks, new ReentrantLock())` | `new` is evaluated once: **one** lock shared by all accounts, i.e. a hidden global lock (reentrant, so it can never deadlock) | `Arrays.setAll(locks, i -> new ReentrantLock())` |

Runs with the buggy versions (not used in the comparison):

- Bug 2 (old `OrderedLockEngine`, synchronized on `Long`): **deadlock**. Prediction: either a broken invariant or a deadlock.
- Bug 4 (`UnorderedLockEngine` with one shared lock, `--accounts=10 --work=20`): `STUCK after 30 s, ok=808,577, deadlocked threads=0`. **Not a deadlock**: a global lock that was simply too slow for the 30 s watchdog (≈ 37 µs per accepted transfer, against ≈ 29 µs with `synchronized`; one run, cut before the end: an observation, not a measurement).

**Lesson:** `STUCK` is a timeout, not a diagnosis. The diagnosis is `deadlocked threads` (`ThreadMXBean.findDeadlockedThreads()`) or `jstack`.

### Experiment: where is the ceiling? (`naive`, 100,000 accounts, `--work=20`)

**Hypothesis:** if the low gain of per-account locks comes from contention, it should almost vanish with 100,000 accounts. The 100,000-account `orderedLock` run says it does not: only ≈ 2 transfers in flight. To find out whether the locks are the limit, measure an engine with **no lock at all** under the same conditions.

| Run | Time | Accepted | Accepted/s | Gap |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 12,726 ms | 991,651 | 77,923 | −109 |
| 2 | 12,435 ms | 991,546 | 79,738 | −639 |
| 3 | 12,477 ms | 991,418 | 79,459 | +116 |

**Median: 79,459/s**, only 8% more than `orderedLock` (73,633/s). Invariant `BROKEN` in 3 runs out of 3, with a random sign.

### Reading the throughput with Little's law

Transfers in flight on average = throughput × time of one transfer (≈ 28.1 µs of work):

| Engine (`--work=20`) | Accepted/s | In flight | Share of the bench ceiling (≈ 80,000/s) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Global lock | 34,500 | ≈ 1.0 | ≈ 43% |
| Ordered, 10 accounts | 28,044 | ≈ 0.8 | ≈ 35% |
| Ordered, 1,000 accounts | 43,833 | ≈ 1.2 | ≈ 55% |
| Ordered, 100,000 accounts | 73,633 | ≈ 2.1 | ≈ 93% |
| Naive (no lock), 100,000 accounts | 79,459 | ≈ 2.2 | 100% |

### Findings

- **Ordering removes deadlocks, not waiting.** With 10 accounts, the ordered engine is **19% slower than a single lock** (≈ 0.8 transfers in flight). A thread holding account 3 while waiting for account 7 blocks everyone who wants account 3 (*hold and wait* is still there), and each transfer needs two lock handovers instead of one (≈ 35.7 µs per accepted transfer, against ≈ 29 µs).
- **Per-account locks pay off only when contention is low:** 28,000 → 44,000 → 74,000/s for 10 → 1,000 → 100,000 accounts. With 1,000 threads and 1,000 accounts, 2,000 locks are wanted for 1,000 accounts: almost every account is contended.
- **Without work, the global lock wins** (3.7 M vs 2.4 M/s at 10 accounts): with almost nothing to do under the lock, taking two locks costs more than the parallelism brings, and parallelism is limited by the 10 cores, not by the number of accounts. Runs of 200–350 ms: order of magnitude only.
- **The bench has its own ceiling: ≈ 80,000/s at `--work=20` with platform threads**, whatever the engine (≈ 2 threads really active at once). Without contention, per-account locks cost only ≈ 7%. Hypothesis: the ceiling comes from OS thread wake-ups (each `parkNanos` puts a real OS thread to sleep); to be checked with `--virtual` on Friday. Every comparison this week must be read against this ceiling, not against the theoretical 500 pairs in parallel.
- **A rare race is still a guaranteed bug:** with 100,000 accounts, the naive engine broke the invariant in 3 runs out of 3, by a few hundred out of 500 M.
- **The JVM detects nothing:** in week 1, PostgreSQL detected ≈ 290 deadlocks and killed one transaction each time (150 s instead of forever). Here, 13 transfers out of 900,000 went through, then the program would have waited forever.
- **Compare accepted transfers per second, not durations.** Rejected transfers and skipped `from == to` pairs (≈ 10% with 10 accounts) do not call `Work.simulate()`, so runs with more accounts last longer only because they accept more transfers (≈ 29 µs per accepted transfer in every global-lock configuration).
- Without `--audit`, the global lock gives the same throughput as on Tuesday at `--work=20` (≈ 34,500/s): the audit cost nothing measurable.
